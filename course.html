Teaching Portfolio

Four formats, one argument:
make the thinking inspectable.

These are working teaching artifacts, not course descriptions. Each one is a different format because it answers a different question — whether a learner can do the thing, whether a room of decision-makers will fund the thing, or whether a group can diagnose why a change stalled.

Every artifact ships as a single self-contained HTML file: readable by a person, parseable by an agent, and versionable in git. That constraint is itself part of what is being taught.

How to read this page

The formats differ on purpose

Pick by what you want to see. The four formats sit at different points of a delivery arc — from a self-serve unit a learner clicks through alone, to a case a facilitator runs with a room.

Interactive Unit

Learner clicks through alone. Self-paced, stepped, no facilitator. Success = they can perform the task afterward.

Lecture Spine

What gets said, and what leaves the room. The content backbone of a live talk — every section pinned to a takeaway for two audience tiers.

Teaching Brief

One argument, worked through. A short position piece that becomes the source material for slides, workshops and reading groups.

Teaching Case

A room argues about a decision. HBS-style: exhibits, no verdict. Built for discussion, not for reading alone.
