This report synthesizes Session B deliverables and 30-day action roadmaps across 11 teams, organized around four change management frameworks: ADKAR for team progress, Kotter for the group roadmap, Prosci stakeholder tools for assessment, and IBM's five principles for AI-specific dynamics. Qualitative findings are derived from self-reported readiness, not rankings.

Executive Summary · Three Core Findings
Course design maps directly to IBM's AI change principles. 8 of 11 teams reached ADKAR's Ability. The shared bottleneck across the group is not skill, but Reinforcement — landing metrics and human intercept checkpoints.

① This course went beyond tool tutorials. Mapping to IBM (2025) principles in hindsight shows that the 3-line summaries, 2×2 critical engagement matrix, accountability tests, and final human authority gates share the same structure (§02). Group A is already driving change with standard global frameworks. ② The ADKAR progress matrix (§05) shows most teams stuck at Reinforcement: teams can ship a demo, but "who intercepts errors, what numbers to track, and who owns risk" remain theoretical. ③ Running 11 initiatives alongside existing digital transformations creates change saturation — we advise HQ Planning to pace deployment via a shared platform (§08).

Consultant Advice

The shared pain point across 11 teams is not building a demo, but "shipping a demo without knowing who catches errors or what numbers count as success." The next steps require only three things: stop tracking prompt counts or usage volume, and measure actual time saved and caught errors; clean documents into AI-readable formats before buying heavy knowledge management suites; and formally empower process rebuilders identified in this workshop to restructure workflows.

§01Methodology Architecture · Framework Breakdown
ADKAR (Hiatt, 2006) — The Spine

Change happens at the individual level. The five workshop modules map directly: Session A = Awareness, Red-Team Dual Inquiry = Desire (self-persuasion), NotebookLM build = Knowledge, Demo delivery = Ability, 30-day list + impact dashboard = Reinforcement.

Kotter (1996) — The Roadmap

Organization-wide 8-step roadmap. This workshop covered steps 1–6 (§07). Steps 7–8 (consolidating gains, anchoring in culture) define the next phase. Using Kotter alongside ADKAR is Prosci standard practice: Kotter guides the organization, ADKAR guides individual milestones.

Prosci Stakeholder Tools — Assessment

Classifies stakeholders by impact × proximity to identify sponsors and build change teams. Supports 4 diagnostics: change impact, readiness, culture, and change saturation (§08 is critical for this group).

IBM AI Change Principles (2025) — The AI Lens

Key differences in AI transformation: start with "Why", anticipate resistance, treat AI as a capability rather than a project, balance governance with speed, and measure outcomes rather than activity. §02 compares these with our workshop design.

Consultant Advice

Methodologies align internal teams; when reporting to leadership, keep it plain: The bottleneck is never whether the model is smart enough, but unorganized documents and missing human guardrails. The first four steps got everyone aware, willing, and capable of shipping a demo. For the next 30 to 90 days, the only priority is making defensive checkpoints and outcome tracking real.

§08Change Saturation · Group Risk Advisory
Risk: 11 Teams in Parallel + Existing Digital Transformation

Prosci's Change Saturation diagnostic warns: individuals have a ceiling for concurrent change. Teams already carry active digital transformation projects; frontline and middle managers (critical for adoption) bear overlapping pressure. Saturation shows up not as open resistance, but superficial compliance — leading to 2×2 Q4 "performative expansion".

Solution: Pace Rollout via Shared Platform

HQ Planning's component pipeline acts as a pacing valve: centralize rollout timing decisions in the pipeline rather than letting subsidiaries start uncoordinated projects. Audit portfolio saturation quarterly (active changes × audience overlap); queue projects when thresholds are exceeded.

Consultant Advice

Saturation manifests as superficial compliance from frontline managers. Every AI project must define ROI thresholds and sunset conditions before launch — if expected results aren't measured within 30 days, sunset it immediately. Land a few solid projects rather than jamming dozens of parallel trials.

§09Next Phase Recommendations (30 → 90 Days)
① Fix R: One "Interception Record" Per Team in 3 Months

Reinforcement is not launching a dashboard, but recording the first real error catch: Quality Agent evaluator rejecting an AI recommendation, Consumer Finance catching a handwriting error, or Logistics reviewing an exception claim. Require one documented catch per team in 90 days — the MVP of measuring impact over activity.

② Formalize Change Management Team & Sponsors

Per Prosci: appoint executive sponsors with public backing; form the change team from HQ Security, HQ Planning, and cluster reps, complete ADKAR training, and manage cross-unit reuse (Kotter Step 7).

③ Differentiated Support for Low-Readiness Teams

Rebuild Desire for Auto Unit B via 1-on-1 interviews; secure legal clearance for Distribution before expanding models — shadow tools won't wait; every week official tools lag, risks multiply.

④ Conduct Formal Assessments (Prosci 4 Tools)

This report's heatmap is qualitative; follow up with formal change impact and culture diagnostics, establishing quarterly saturation reviews for change tracking.

Consultant Advice

The 90-day focus: Track "evidence of the first real error intercept", not dashboard launch dates (e.g., evaluator rejecting an AI prompt, or loan officer catching a flawed OCR). Identify seed champions who actively restructured workflows and give them cross-department mandate. Official paths must be faster and more accurate than private workarounds, or shadow workflows will persist.
