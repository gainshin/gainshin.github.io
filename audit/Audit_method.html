The one-sentence version

The AI medical product now writes more carefully than it reasons. The vagueness that removed the visible defects — "the state" instead of a state, "your mom" instead of a bound entity — is the same vagueness that makes the output unverifiable, and it is produced by a system that still cannot read its own profile.

§1

Method: what I changed before re-running

The first audit could be dismissed on one ground: the profile contained only a spouse, so a plan about a spouse was arguably correct behaviour on bad data. This run removes that defence by manipulating the profile first, then re-running the identical probe.

Two manipulations, two hypotheses

Add a parent as care receiverIf the correct entity exists, does the plan bind to it?Could not perform. No surface in the product allows adding or editing a care receiver. See §2.

Out of scope. One account, one probe, one session. I did not test other scenario prompts, other locales, the French surface, mobile, or whether behaviour differs for accounts whose profile is more complete. Where I could not verify something it is named as unverified rather than asserted.

§2 · New finding

N-01Critical

The roster every plan is built on cannot be edited

I set out to add a mother to the profile. There is no way to do it. Care receivers appear in the Sharing Hub as a read-only tree, and nowhere in the product is there a control to add, rename, re-relate or remove one.

Surfaces checked, in this order: main menu, /profile (Account Settings), /organizer (Sharing Hub — Stories and Assistants tabs), /gather-assist including all eleven steps, /user-onboarding-settings (Manage Steps), and Step 9 "Care Setup", which turns out to contain Medication, Care Providers and Medical Records — not people.

Sharing Hub · verbatim, read-only Care Giver/ Care Manager → Profile: Joshua
Care Receivers → Spouse: Mary
Pets → Dog: Mary's Lucky · Autos → Car: Mary's None · Real Estate → Mary's Mary

The stray values in that tree — "Mary's None", "Mary's Mary" — are visible to the user and cannot be corrected either. They also indicate that everything in the profile is namespaced under a single care receiver, which is what makes the roster load-bearing: it is not one field among many, it is the root of the graph.

Why this outranks the original finding

Run 01 reported that the assistant silently swapped the person. The deeper problem is that a caregiver who notices the swap has no way to fix it. A wrong answer you can correct is a usability defect; a wrong answer you cannot correct is a governance defect.

§3 · New finding

N-02Critical

Jurisdiction is collected, stored, and never read

Run 01 concluded that the product "never asks what state you live in." That was wrong, and the truth is worse. Account Settings has Country, State, City and ZIP. The State field was empty. I set it to New York and saved.

Account Settings · after saveSuccess — state = 4665 (New York) persisted

Then I asked the product's own profile assistant whether it knew:

Search in profile · verbatim Q: "Which state do I live in, and does it affect the benefit rules that apply to me?"
A: "There is no information available about this topic in your profile."

The account store and the assistant's retrieval index are two different places, and nothing joins them. The product asks the user for the single fact that determines whether most of its financial and benefits guidance is valid, writes it to disk, and then generates that guidance without it.

§4 · New finding

N-03Critical

Six clarifying questions, none of them load-bearing

Run 01 saw three clarifying questions. This run got two rounds of three. The assistant is now more inquisitive and no better grounded: every question asks for care detail, and none establishes a condition under which the answer would be valid.

The distinction matters because these two classes of question have different failure costs. Getting a care-detail question wrong makes a plan less useful. Getting a validity-condition question wrong — who, where — makes the entire plan wrong while leaving it looking right. The assistant spends all six of its questions on the cheap class.

It then declares sufficiency in its own words:

Turn 3 · verbatim"I have enough to build personalized recommendations for your situation."

The word doing the work there is personalized. The system has no person bound, no jurisdiction, and cannot read the one profile record it does hold.

§5

N-04Minor

A promise the loading state does not keep

The generation step announces a duration, then quietly abandons it. There is no cancel, no progress detail, and no failure path visible to the user.

Minor in isolation. It matters here because the stated context is a caregiver with a two-day decision window, and because the copy switches from a measured claim to reassurance — "Just a little longer. Thanks for your patience." — which is the register a product uses when it no longer knows how long something will take.

§6 · The main change since run 01

N-05Critical

Three mutually exclusive futures, all ticked

Run 01 found a flat 30-item list with no priority layer. That has genuinely changed: output is now organised into three named scenarios, each with Considerations and Tasks. The structure is a real improvement. What it is used for is not.

The three scenarios are not complementary. They are the actual decision the caregiver came to make:

  • Initial Home Support and Transition Planning — informal methods, apps, family assistance
  • Formalizing Home Care with Legal and Professional Support — power of attorney, paid care manager
  • Exploring Facility-Based Long-Term Care — assisted living or skilled nursing

A person choosing the third does not also do the first. Yet every group arrives fully checked — 7 of 7, 8 of 8, 8 of 8, 8 of 8, 8 of 8, 7 of 7 — and the footer reads 46 items selected, one button away from becoming 46 tasks in the account.

The product generated a decision and then declined to present it as one. Adding the scenario layer without adding selection semantics makes this worse than the flat list it replaced: the flat list was obviously undifferentiated, whereas this looks like considered options while behaving like a checklist.

The smallest fix

Default the scenarios to none selected and make choosing one an explicit act. The generation work is already done and it is good; only the default is wrong. A caregiver who picks "Facility-Based" and gets 15 items has been helped. One who saves 46 has been handed the problem back, larger.

§7 · New finding

N-06Major

Fifteen sources, zero links

Each scenario carries a "Sources 5" control. Expanded, it lists five named references. None of the fifteen is a hyperlink — a DOM query for anchors returned zero across the whole document.

The named institutions are real and appropriate: AARP, the Eldercare Locator, NCOA, the American Geriatrics Society, NAELA, the Alzheimer's Association, CFPB, HHS. That is the problem. A list of respected names that cannot be opened performs the function of evidence without doing the work of it, and a caregiver has no way to tell a correctly attributed claim from an invented one.

One entry deserves specific attention. The facility scenario cites the Genworth Cost of Care Survey — a source whose entire content is cost figures broken down by state — inside a plan that never established which state applies, generated by a system that could not read the state I had just saved.

§8

What got fixed, and what only got quieter

Both Critical findings from run 01 failed to reproduce. Read carefully, that is not the same as either being resolved.

Run 01 findingRun 02 symptomCauseRead
F-01 · Plan built about the wrong person Did not reproduce. Zero mentions of "Mary" or "spouse"; the plan is about "your mom". Unaddressed. The plan is correct because the conversation said "mom" six times, not because an entity was bound. The profile still holds only a spouse, the plan still carries no "For:" attribution, and the roster still cannot be edited. Masked
F-02 · State-determined figures presented as national facts Did not reproduce. No CSRA, no MMMNA, no dollar figures anywhere. Unaddressed. Replaced by avoidance: "Contact the state Department of Aging". The state is known to the account and unknown to the assistant. Masked
F-03 · 30 items, all pre-selected, no first step Now 46 items in three named scenarios with Considerations and Tasks. Partially addressed. Real structural improvement; selection semantics not added. All 46 still arrive checked, across options that are alternatives. Regressed

The pattern across all three is one thing: the output moved toward vagueness and the grounding stayed where it was. Vagueness is a legitimate short-term safety measure — a plan that says "the state" cannot cite the wrong state's numbers. It is not a fix, and it has a cost the first audit did not have to price: an answer general enough to be safe is also general enough to be useless to someone deciding in two days.

Worth protecting

  • The scenario decomposition is genuinely good work. The three options are the right three.
  • Considerations-then-Tasks is the correct shape: reasons before actions.
  • The explicit "Show Recommendations" gate is new and right — it separates gathering from generating.
  • The persistent footer, "AI medical product can make mistakes; please double-check its responses.", is present on the output surface.

§9

What a runtime audit layer would have caught here

Every finding in this document is a binding failure: a fact the system holds, or should hold, that never reaches the thing generating the answer. None of them is visible in a compliance document, and none is detectable by checking whether a disclosure component exists on screen.

CheckClassVerdict this run
Subject bindingEntityUnchecked — no entity was ever declared or displayed
Jurisdiction bindingEntityGap — stored value not used, not surfaced as unknown
Validity-condition coverageElicitationGap — 0 of 6 questions established a validity condition
Source-use divergenceCitationGap — 15 named sources, none resolvable, none traceable to a claim
Option exclusivityDecisionGap — mutually exclusive scenarios all pre-selected
Disclosure prominenceDisclosurePresent — persistent footer, weight untested

Five of six are gaps, and all five are mechanically checkable at the moment the answer is produced: an unbound subject, an unused stored field, an unresolvable citation, and a set of options where every alternative is selected are all detectable without domain reasoning. That is the argument for the layer, made on one real product rather than in the abstract — and it is why this class of product, not cross-device agents, is the right place to keep digging.

Caveat on the last row. "Present" is not "effective". Whether the footer disclosure is read, and whether anything in this interface would change a caregiver's confidence, is exactly the question the trust-and-reasoning study exists to answer, and it is not answered here.
